Handmade Web - J.R. Carpender

Reading A Handmade Web made me realize how little I think about the internet as something I can actually create myself. I'm so used to going on Instagram, TikTok, or other platforms that I never really question who controls the space I'm using. I found Carpenter's idea of the handmade web interesting because it reminded me that the internet can also be personal and experimental.
The idea that websites don't always need to be perfect or follow the same design standards makes me feel less overwhelmed about creating my own website. I like the idea of something feeling handmade and personal because you can see the person behind it. It makes me feel like I don't have to make my website perfect, but instead can let it reflect my own personality and creativity. It can always grow over time.

9/1

A Rant on Technology - Ursula K. Le Guin

How often do we treat technology as something separate from people. This reading made me think about how technology is actually everywhere in our everyday lives. When I think of technology, I usually think of computers, phones, or new inventions. But things like a cup, a pen, or even writing were also technologies at some point. They were new ways of doing something, but because we have used them for so long, they no longer feel like technology to us.
This made me realize how surrounded we are by technology without really noticing it. Almost everything we use has some kind of advancement, design, or technology behind it. I think technology becomes almost invisible when we become familiar with it. We stop seeing it as an invention and just see it as part of everyday life. It made me wonder what things we use today that future generations might see as old or basic technology.

9/1

My Website is a Shifting House... - Laurel Schwulst

The metaphor of a website as a garden was the one I liked the most. I like the idea that, just like a garden, a website can have its own seasons. There can be times when it is active and full of new things, and there can also be quieter periods when not much is happening. I think that is perfectly fine.
It also made me think about how we expect websites to always be updated and active, when sometimes it is natural for things to slow down. A garden has its time to bloom and its time to be quiet, and I like thinking of a website in the same way. It can have its ups and downs, grow over time, and still be meaningful even when nothing new is being added.
