Head of Buddha

Afghanistan (probably Hadda)
Date: 5th-6th century
Medium: Stucco with traces of paint
Dimensions: H. 7 1/2 in. (19.1 cm); W. 4 3/4 in. (12.1 cm); D. 4 1/2 in. (11.4 cm)
Classification: Sculpture
Credit Line: Rogers Fund, 1930, The MET

The heavy, lowered eyelids signify a state of deep meditation (dhyana). The eyes are completely relaxed but not entirely shut, indicating that while the Buddha is turned inward toward ultimate spiritual truth, he remains ever-aware of the world and extends boundless compassion to all sentient beings.
The completely relaxed facial musculature and the faint, bow-shaped smile convey absolute equanimity. This perfectly calm face reflects the achievement of Nirvana, a state completely free from desire, ignorance, anger, and the endless cycle of suffering
